# ReadLark tweets — week 2026-W42

## 2026-10-12 (Mon) — 1% compounding
1. You don't fall behind in a day. You fall behind 1% at a time, so quietly you never notice — until the gap is a canyon. The same math builds you back up. One rep at a time.
2. Try this: name your 1%. Not the heroic version — the smallest improvement you could repeat on your worst day. One page. One lap. One line. Then just protect the streak. 🧵
3. Be honest: which habit of yours is compounding the wrong way right now? The tiny daily thing you keep telling yourself is "fine."

## 2026-10-13 (Tue) — identity-based habits
1. "I'm trying to quit" keeps you a smoker fighting an urge. "I'm not a smoker" means you already became someone else. Habits change when identity does, not before.
2. Reframe the goal: the point was never to read a book — it was to become a reader. Before any choice, ask "what would that person do?" then do that one thing. It's a vote.
3. Who are you quietly becoming, one small action at a time — on purpose, or by accident?

## 2026-10-14 (Wed) — the two-minute rule
1. The hardest part of any habit isn't the doing. It's the starting. Shrink the start to two minutes and you remove the only wall that actually stops you.
2. Scale it down until skipping feels silly. Not "run 3 miles" — put on your shoes. Not "write the essay" — write one sentence. Master showing up before you optimize. 🧵
3. What habit have you been putting off because the "real" version feels too big to start?

## 2026-10-15 (Thu) — environment design
1. You don't need more willpower. You need a better room. People with great self-control mostly just spend less time near temptation. They designed it out.
2. Engineer friction both ways: book on the pillow, phone charging in another room, app deleted, fruit on the counter. Make the good choice the easy one. #habits
3. Look around the room you're in. What's making the wrong thing easy — and what one thing could you move today?

## 2026-10-16 (Fri) — never miss twice
1. You will miss a day. That's just arithmetic. The danger was never the missed day — it's the story that one slip "ruins it." Missing once is an accident. Twice starts a new habit.
2. Make the comeback tiny. Don't repay the lost day — just do the two-minute version and cast the next vote. Guard the day *after* a slip like it's the whole habit. 🧵
3. What's your plan for the next time life interrupts your streak — before it happens?
