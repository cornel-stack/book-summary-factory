# PerCuriam tweets — week 2026-W42

## 2026-10-12 (Mon) — security deposits
1. Myth: a landlord can keep your deposit for "normal wear and tear" like faded paint. Usually they can't. You pay for damage, not for the simple fact that you lived there. (varies by state)
2. Protect your deposit: photograph every room at move-in AND move-out, dated. That turns a "he said, she said" into proof. Most US states also require an itemized list of any deductions. 🧵
3. Ever had a deposit kept for something that felt like ordinary wear? What was the "deduction"? (general info, not legal advice)

## 2026-10-13 (Tue) — at-will employment
1. "They can't fire me without a good reason" — in most US states, they actually can. At-will means almost any reason, or none. The line is: not an *illegal* one.
2. Illegal reasons still exist: firing based on race, sex, religion, age, disability, or in retaliation for reporting wrongdoing. Contracts and handbooks can also change the default. 🧵
3. Did you know your job was at-will when you took it — or did you assume you could only be fired "for cause"? (general info, not legal advice)

## 2026-10-14 (Wed) — debt collectors (FDCPA)
1. A collector threatening you with arrest over an unpaid credit card bill is likely breaking federal law. The FDCPA bans threats, lies, and calls before 8am or after 9pm.
2. Two quiet powers under the FDCPA: demand written verification of the debt, and send a written cease-contact letter. Keep copies of everything. #FDCPA 🧵
3. Have you ever gotten a collection call that felt like it crossed a line? (general info, not legal advice)

## 2026-10-15 (Thu) — small claims court
1. You usually don't need a lawyer to sue over a few thousand dollars — small claims court was built for exactly that. In many states lawyers aren't even allowed.
2. Prep wins cases: bring organized docs, receipts, photos, texts, and a plain timeline. And remember — winning a judgment and collecting the money are two different steps. 🧵
3. What small-money dispute have you let slide because you assumed court wasn't worth it? (dollar limits vary by state; general info, not legal advice)

## 2026-10-16 (Fri) — warranty of habitability
1. "The lease never promised heat." In most US states, it didn't have to. The warranty of habitability requires landlords to keep a home livable — heat, water, plumbing — even if the lease is silent.
2. If something essential breaks: tell your landlord in writing, be specific, give reasonable time. Remedies like repair-and-deduct exist but have strict rules — follow your state's closely. 🧵
